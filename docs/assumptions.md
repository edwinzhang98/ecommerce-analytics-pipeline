# Assumptions

What the marts take as true that the source does not state outright. Each row gives the evidence, what
would move if the assumption is wrong, and where it is encoded, so a change starts in a known place. Numbers
are from the full extract as built on 2026-11-03.

**Sources.** *Olist docs* is the dataset description published with the Kaggle release
([olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce)). *Data* means
measured on the extract; `DQ-xx` points to the [issue register](../sql/data_quality/issue_register.yml) and the
[data quality report](data_quality_report.md). *Decision* is a modelling choice, recorded in the ADR or document
linked in the row.

**Changing an assumption.** Edit what the last column names and run `make build`; a seed change that touches a
terminal status also needs a full refresh of `fct_order_items` ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-incremental-fct-order-items.md)).
A-02 and A-12 define net orders and A-14 net GMV, so changing them needs the Analytics Lead and Finance sign-off
([01_request_and_scope.md](01_request_and_scope.md)). Any other change to a published metric goes through the
Analytics Lead ([how to change a definition](02_metric_definitions.md#14-owner-and-how-to-change-a-definition)).
Model names refer to [`dbt/models/`](../dbt/models/); their columns are in the [data dictionary](data_dictionary.md).

## Where the headline numbers are most sensitive

| Assumption | Headline number | If the assumption flips |
|---|---|---|
| A-02 date basis | net orders per month | Booked on delivery, 36,427 net orders (37%) move to a later month |
| A-10 repeat customer | repeat rate 2.17% | 2.78% on distinct checkouts, 3.04% on orders |
| A-12 open orders are net | net orders 98,207 | 1,729 lower (1.76%) without open orders; 1,725 of them were past their estimated delivery date by 2018-09-03 |
| A-14 freight is revenue | net GMV R$15,735,527.03 | 14.24% lower without freight |
| A-19 bad review = 1-2 stars | bad-review rate 9.25% on time, 62.42% late (Q1) | 17.32% and 73.30% with 3 stars counted as bad |
| A-20 late = a later calendar day | late rate 6.77% | 8.11% if arriving on the estimated day counted as late |

| A-02 | Order metrics are dated by the local purchase date (`order_purchase_date`), not by approval or delivery. | **Decision** ([ADR 0002](adr/0002-order-status-and-net-orders.md)): the purchase is the customer's decision and the only lifecycle event recorded for every order (approval is missing on 160 orders, delivery on 2,965). | Booked on delivery, 36,427 net orders (37%) would fall in a later month; booked on approval, 1,656. | `int_orders_classified` → `fct_orders`, `fct_order_items`; `agg_time_dimension` in [`_semantic_models.yml`](../dbt/models/semantic/_semantic_models.yml) |

## Customers and grain

| ID | Assumption | Why / source | Impact if wrong | Encoded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-06 | A person is `customer_unique_id`. The source `customer_id` is issued per order and is not exposed in the marts. | **Olist docs:** every order gets its own `customer_id`; `customer_unique_id` exists to find repeat buyers. **Data:** 99,441 `customer_id`s belong to 96,096 people, 2,997 of whom have 2+ (DQ-01); on `customer_id` the repeat rate is 0%. | If `customer_unique_id` itself splits or merges people, customer counts and repeat rates are off by an amount the extract cannot show. | `int_customers_aggregated_to_persons` → keys of `dim_customers`, `customer_360`; `fct_orders` carries no `customer_id` |
| A-07 | The customer record of an order (zip, city, state) is that order's delivery address. | **Olist docs:** the customers file is where each order's delivery location is found. **Data:** 252 people have orders at 2+ addresses (39 in 2+ states), so the record follows the order, not the person. | If it were a registered home address, `fct_orders.delivery_state` and the delivery-by-state analysis (Q1) would describe where customers are registered, not where parcels went. | `int_orders_joined` → `fct_orders.delivery_zip_code_prefix`, `delivery_city`, `delivery_state`, `delivery_region` |
| A-08 | A person's current location is the address of their latest net order (their latest order if they have no net order). | **Decision** ([ADR 0003](adr/0003-grain-fan-out-and-customer-identity.md)): a canceled order's address should not win, and the latest net order is the best evidence of where someone lives now. | Only people with 2+ addresses are affected: the order's zip prefix differs from the person's for 269 orders, the state for 43. | `int_customers_aggregated_to_persons` → `dim_customers.customer_zip_code_prefix`, `customer_city`, `customer_state`, coordinates |
| A-09 | One `order_items` row is one unit; quantity is the number of rows. | **Olist docs:** `order_item_id` numbers the items in an order. **Data:** there is no quantity column, 6,968 orders repeat a product on 2+ rows (7,088 order-product pairs, DQ-04), and the rows add up to what was paid (A-14). | If a row could hold several units, unit counts would be understated; GMV would not, since it reconciles with payments. | Grain of `stg_olist__order_items` and `fct_order_items`; uniqueness test on `order_id` + `order_item_id` |
| A-10 | A repeat customer has net orders on 2+ distinct purchase days; several orders on one day are one shopping occasion. | **Decision** ([ADR 0003](adr/0003-grain-fan-out-and-customer-identity.md)). **Data:** 290 times a person placed 2+ orders in the same second (582 orders), often one per seller, which looks like a split checkout, not a return visit (DQ-05). | If same-day orders were separate decisions, the repeat rate would be 2.78% (distinct checkouts) or 3.04% (orders) instead of 2.17%. | `purchase_days` in `int_customers_aggregated_to_persons` → `dim_customers.is_repeat_customer`; `customer_360.days_to_second_purchase` |

## Order status and the order KPI

| ID | Assumption | Why / source | Impact if wrong | Encoded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-11 | Statuses follow one lifecycle, created → approved → invoiced → processing → shipped → delivered, with canceled and unavailable as exits. Delivered, canceled and unavailable are terminal. | **Olist docs** give examples (delivered, shipped) but no definitions. **Data:** each status has its stage's timestamps: all 1,107 shipped orders have a carrier pickup and no delivery, invoiced and processing orders are approved but not picked up, and the 5 created orders are not approved. | A wrongly grouped status moves orders between net and non-net; a terminal status that can still change breaks the incremental rule (A-28). | Seed [`order_status_mapping.csv`](../dbt/seeds/order_status_mapping.csv): `order_status_group`, `is_terminal_status`, `status_sort_order` |
| A-12 | A net order is any order that is not canceled or unavailable, including the 1,729 open orders (created, approved, invoiced, processing, shipped). | **Decision** ([ADR 0002](adr/0002-order-status-and-net-orders.md)), owned by the Analytics Lead with Finance sign-off: it counts what customers ordered and the business has not lost. Placed 99,441, net 98,207, delivered 96,478. | 1,725 open orders were past their estimated delivery date by 2018-09-03 and may never complete, so net orders overstate fulfilled orders by up to 1.76%. If Finance books on delivery, the KPI becomes delivered orders. | Seed column `is_net_order` → `fct_orders.is_net_order`; metric `net_orders` |
| A-13 | Lifecycle timestamps that contradict each other are recording or process quirks, not invalid orders: the orders are kept and flagged, never dropped. | **Data:** 1,410 orders (1.42%) have 1+ contradiction, most often a carrier pickup before payment approval (1,359) (DQ-14). | Durations on these orders are unreliable. If some were invalid orders, net orders would be overstated by at most 1,404 (the net orders among them). | `int_orders_classified` → `fct_orders.has_lifecycle_anomaly`, `data_quality_flags`; 7 lifecycle `known_issue__*` tests |

## Money

| ID | Assumption | Why / source | Impact if wrong | Encoded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-14 | Revenue is GMV, item price plus freight; net GMV is the GMV of net orders. | **Decision** ([ADR 0004](adr/0004-payments-cancellations-and-refunds.md)). **Olist docs:** an order's freight is split across its items. **Data:** payments are within one cent of items + freight for 98,362 of 98,665 orders. | If revenue should exclude freight, net GMV is overstated by 14.24% (R$2,241,126.29 of R$15,735,527.03). | `order_gmv_brl` in `int_order_items_aggregated_to_orders` → `fct_orders`; `fct_order_items.item_total_brl`; metric `net_gmv_brl` |
| A-15 | Card payments in installments include interest, so `payment_total_brl` can exceed GMV. Payments are kept separately and never used as revenue. | **Data:** 229 of the 232 orders overpaid by more than R$1 are card payments in installments; the net gap is R$2,870.39 (0.02%) (DQ-16). The source has no interest field. | If the excess were fees or errors, only the reading of `payment_gmv_difference_brl` changes; GMV does not depend on payments. | `fct_orders.payment_total_brl`, `payment_gmv_difference_brl`, `is_payment_mismatch`; var `payment_mismatch_tolerance_brl` (R$1.00) |
| A-16 | Canceled and unavailable orders earn no revenue, and no order is treated as refunded: the source has no refund or chargeback data. | **Decision** ([ADR 0004](adr/0004-payments-cancellations-and-refunds.md)). **Data:** all 1,234 canceled and unavailable orders keep their payment rows (R$269,735.11) but hold only R$108,026.21 of item GMV; 6 canceled orders have a delivery timestamp. | Net GMV is bookings, not cash. Refunds after delivery would leave it overstated by an unknown amount, and money kept on canceled orders (up to R$269,735.11) would be missing. | `is_net_order` filter in net GMV; `payment_total_brl` kept unadjusted |

## Reviews and delivery

| ID | Assumption | Why / source | Impact if wrong | Encoded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-17 | `review_creation_date` is the day the satisfaction survey was sent, not when the customer answered. | **Olist docs** describe it as the survey send date. **Data:** it carries no time of day (00:00, or 01:00 on the two DST start days), and 64 surveys are dated before the purchase (DQ-15). | Only review-latency analysis depends on it; no mart metric uses it. | Renamed `review_survey_sent_date` in `stg_olist__order_reviews`; the answer time is `review_answered_at` |
| A-18 | When an order has several survey answers, the most recently answered one is its review; `review_count` keeps the number. | **Decision** ([ADR 0003](adr/0003-grain-fan-out-and-customer-identity.md)): the latest answer is the customer's final opinion. **Data:** 547 orders have 2-3 answers, and 202 of them disagree on the score (DQ-03). | Keeping the first answer instead would flip the bad-review flag on 96 of 98,673 reviewed orders. | `int_reviews_deduplicated_to_orders`; unit test `test_review_dedup_keeps_latest_answer` |
| A-19 | A bad review is a score of 1 or 2 stars. | **Decision** ([metric definitions 6.1](02_metric_definitions.md#61-bad-review-rate)): 3 is the neutral point of the 1-5 scale. **Data:** 14,494 of 98,673 reviewed orders (14.69%) score 1-2. | Counting 3 stars as bad raises the rates (Q1: 17.32% on time, 73.30% late, against 9.25% and 62.42%); the late vs on-time gap stays (56 against 53 percentage points). | Var `bad_review_max_score` (2) → `fct_orders.is_bad_review`; a separate SQL variable of the same name in [`q1_delivery_vs_reviews_by_state.sql`](../analyses/q1_delivery_vs_reviews_by_state.sql) (keep them equal) |
| A-20 | A delivery is late when it arrives on a later calendar day than the estimated delivery date; arriving on that day is on time. | **Olist docs:** the estimate is the date shown to the customer at purchase. **Data:** it has no time of day in any of the 99,441 orders, so the comparison is by calendar day. | Counting the estimated day as late would raise the late rate from 6.77% to 8.11% (1,292 more orders). | `int_orders_classified.is_late_delivery`; unit test `test_late_delivery_and_status_logic` |
| A-21 | Delivery metrics exist only for delivered orders with a delivery timestamp. | **Data:** 8 delivered orders lack the timestamp and 6 canceled orders have one (DQ-14); both are excluded, leaving 96,470 measured deliveries. | Negligible for those 14 orders. The late rate also leaves out the 1,107 orders still shipped at extract time; 1,103 were past their estimate by 2018-09-03, and counting them as late gives 7.83%. | `int_orders_classified.has_delivery_metrics` → `delivery_days`, `delivery_delay_days`, `is_late_delivery` (null otherwise) |

## Geography and reference data

| ID | Assumption | Why / source | Impact if wrong | Encoded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-22 | A coordinate outside the box latitude -34.0 to 5.5, longitude -74.5 to -28.5 (Brazil and its Atlantic islands) is wrong and is dropped. | **Data:** 31 geolocation rows fall outside, as far out as latitude 45.07 and longitude 121.11 (DQ-10). | The box is coarse: a wrong point inside Brazil survives, but the per-prefix median (A-23) outvotes isolated strays. | Macro [`is_within_brazil`](../dbt/macros/is_within_brazil.sql) → `stg_olist__geolocation.is_within_brazil` |

After de-duplication the median prefix still has 22 points, and a median ignores strays that a mean would follow. | Coordinates are prefix centroids, not addresses: fine for maps by state or region, not for door-to-door distance. 269 people (0.28%) have none (DQ-09). | `int_geolocation_aggregated_to_zip_prefixes` → coordinates in `dim_customers`, `dim_sellers` |
| A-24 | When a seller's typed city is unusable, the most common city of the seller's zip prefix replaces it. | **Data:** 7 sellers entered a zip code, an e-mail address or a state code as their city (DQ-17); all 7 prefixes resolve to a city. | At most 7 seller cities are wrong; the state comes from the seller record and is unaffected. | `dim_sellers.seller_city`, `is_city_inferred_from_zip`; macro [`clean_city_name`](../dbt/macros/clean_city_name.sql); unit test `test_seller_city_cleaning` |
| A-25 | A product without a category gets `unknown`, a bucket of its own; the 2 categories missing from the translation file get English names from a seed. | **Data:** 610 products have no category (DQ-07): 1,589 net-order items, 1.42% of items and 1.31% of net GMV. `pc_gamer` and `portateis_cozinha_e_preparadores_de_alimentos` lack a translation (DQ-20). | If the unknowns belong mostly to one real category, that category is understated by up to 1.31% of net GMV. | `dim_products.product_category`; seed [`product_category_translation_overrides.csv`](../dbt/seeds/product_category_translation_overrides.csv); test `assert_all_product_categories_translated` |

## Sellers

| ID | Assumption | Why / source | Impact if wrong | Encoded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-26 | In a multi-seller order, order-level outcomes (late delivery, review) count for every seller in it. | **Decision** ([ADR 0003](adr/0003-grain-fan-out-and-customer-identity.md)): delivery and review are recorded once per order, with nothing to say which seller caused them. **Data:** 1,277 net orders (1.3% of net orders with items) have 2+ sellers. | A seller can be credited or blamed for another seller's parcel in 1.3% of orders. Seller GMV is unaffected because it is summed per item. | `seller_scorecard.late_delivery_rate`, `bad_review_rate`, `avg_review_score` |
| A-27 | A handover is late when the carrier pickup comes after the seller's shipping limit; it is measured on single-seller orders only. | **Olist docs:** `shipping_limit_date` is the seller's deadline to hand the order to the carrier, and `order_delivered_carrier_date` is the handover. **Data:** there is one pickup time per order, but sellers in one order can have different deadlines (336 of the 1,278 multi-seller orders do). | Multi-seller orders (1.3%) are left out of the rate. 8 items with a deadline more than 90 days after purchase can only look on time (DQ-12). | `seller_scorecard.late_handover_rate`, `handed_over_orders` |

## Pipeline and tests

| ID | Assumption | Why / source | Impact if wrong | Encoded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-28 | Delivered, canceled and unavailable orders never change status, so incremental runs of `fct_order_items` re-process only recent orders and orders still open at the last run. | **Decision** ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-incremental-fct-order-items.md)); the seed defines which statuses are terminal. The extract cannot confirm it (one snapshot), and 6 canceled orders with a delivery timestamp hint that a change after delivery happens. | A changed terminal order keeps a stale `is_net_order` in `fct_order_items`: `item_net_gmv_reconciles_with_order_net_gmv` fails, `snp_olist__orders` records the change, and `--full-refresh` repairs it. | Incremental filter in [`fct_order_items.sql`](../dbt/models/marts/core/fct_order_items.sql); seed column `is_terminal_status` |
| A-29 | An order's item rows are loaded before `fct_order_items` holds purchases more than 3 days newer than that order. | **Decision** ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-incremental-fct-order-items.md)): the loader stamps every row of a load with one `_loaded_at`, so purchase time is the only usable watermark; 3 days absorbs rows that land a little late. | Later rows are skipped until a full refresh. Tested: 25 orders held back from an earlier extract lose 26 rows, the grain test fails as designed, and `--full-refresh` repairs it. | Var `fct_order_items_lookback_days` in [`dbt_project.yml`](../dbt/dbt_project.yml); test `fct_order_items_has_one_row_per_source_item`; [`check_incremental.py`](../scripts/check_incremental.py) |
| A-30 | For the 13 registered issues that have a count baseline, today's count is accepted: the test passes at that count, warns if it grows and errors if it explodes. | **Decision** (decision points in [01_request_and_scope.md](01_request_and_scope.md)): these issues are documented and handled in the models, so only a change in their size is news. The other 8 are handled by the model design, by hard tests or, for the time zone (DQ-21), by A-01. | If today's level is already harmful, the test stays green until it grows. Raising a baseline is a reviewed change, not a quiet edit. | 24 tests tagged `known_issue` (`warn_if`, `error_if`, `store_failures`); [`issue_register.yml`](../sql/data_quality/issue_register.yml) |

Full-data checks run locally (`make all`, `make incremental-check`). | [`make_fixture.py`](../ci/make_fixture.py), [`check_known_issues_detected.py`](../ci/check_known_issues_detected.py), [`ci.yml`](../.github/workflows/ci.yml) |
